Two step-by-step tutorials, a reference page and a practice exercise for turning an observed need into a question you can study and a project you can run. Use them alongside the field work: the conceptual model sharpens what you think is going on; the logic model plans what you will do about it.

| Page | What it is | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Building a conceptual model](conceptual-model.html) | Seven steps from a focused research question to a labeled model, hypotheses and measures | You can name a need and want to explain what drives it |
| [Building a project logic model](logic-model.html) | Seven steps from partners and evidence to inputs, activities, outcomes, indicators and roles | You are ready to plan an intervention with partners |
| [Terms and deeper dives](terms.html) | Plain-language definitions, a deeper look at research questions, and brief guides to five common health theories | A term or theory in the tutorials is new to you |
| [Sheffield practice exercise](sheffield-exercise.html) | A 20-minute facilitated exercise with synthetic rural diabetes data and a sample model | Rehearsing the conceptual model before the Sheffield rotation |

**Suggested order:** read the conceptual model tutorial, run the Sheffield exercise as a team, then move to the logic model once a need has been framed. Keep the terms page open throughout.

**On data:** everything in the Sheffield exercise is synthetic. Real observations, names and quotes from the field stay in Notion, never in this repository.
